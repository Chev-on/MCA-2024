 ](tasks/Week10.html) # Music Curation and Analytics Portfolio - Vivaldi ## Introduction / Week 1 ### Theme - Vivaldi and his Four Seasons The theme I have chosen for my data set is focused on the collection of pieces commonly known as "The Four Seasons" by the composer Antonio Vivaldi. Antonio Vivaldi was a very successful Italian composer and violin virtuoso in the Baroque era of music (17th - early 18th century). In his time, Vivaldi was a very busy composer and performer and is partially credited for the development of classical music, introducing and changing orchestral playing and violin techniques. In his lifetime Vivaldi composed over 500 concerti most of which were for string orchestra and soloist and solo violin. One of the most famous being a set of four violin concertos known as _Le quattro stagioni_, The Four Seasons[^1]. ## Current Manifestations of Data "Based on your own experience, what do you think are some challenges to working with music and music-related data (perhaps related to access, curation, distribution, etc.)? How does your selected theme display some of these challenges? Describe the current manifestations of data relating to your selected theme, discussing how it is presently curated (collected and gathered) and how it is presented, described, and analysed." - **Access: Limits of Copyright Law** Copyright laws tells us that artistic works are free to the public domain 70 years after the authours lifetime[^2]. This means all of Vivaldi's published works are available for public use, and are easily accessable. However, this does not apply to recordings or arrangements of his pieces. Considering Vivaldi's life ended much before audio recording was even invented (1857/1877)[^3], much of the recordings available today are still under copyright law. This can greatly limit public use and access. - **Curation: Music from the 17th Century** Through the years preservation of the arts has been a consistent challenge for researchers, historians and artists. Whether through years of war or lack of technology much art has been lost to history. Vivaldi wrote many pieces of music throughout his life and it is unknown if there are some which may have been incorrectly archived or misinterpreted. The challange with this is that there is not a sure way of knowing if an interpreted performance is exactly the way Vivaldi intented. One *could* skim through pages and pages of Italian notes and directions but this would be very difficult to do for a 40 minute piece like The Four Seasons. - **Distribution: Music Organisations** Most of the sheet music and audio files I used were sourced from IMSLP, the International Music Score Library Project[^4]. The first piece I will look at within the Four Seasons known as "Summer", specfically the first allegro non molto movement.
I first downloaded the full score from IMSLP.org, however quickly realised that a full orchestral score will be difficult to analyse so decided to choose an arrangement consisting of Violin and Piano.
The full orchestral score is availble here and the arrangement by one Jordi Vilaprinyó is shown below and also available here.

When loading the PDF into musescore one of thie first things I noticed is that information such as the title, author, arranger, tempo were all missing. This information is quite essential, especially when using copyrighted arrangements of pieces.
Musescore did insert a 'ghost' tempo, but it was incorrect to the original. It also misread in pedal signals at various places which is unusal as the original didn't have any.
Directions were also missing such as the 'allegro non molto' and 'Languideza per il caldo'.
